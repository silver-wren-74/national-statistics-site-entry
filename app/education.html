Schooling in Indonesia

Indonesia made primary schooling free and compulsory decades ago, and net enrolment at primary level is 97.7%. The average adult has 9.2 years of schooling (BPS, 2024) — just past junior secondary — while a child entering school today is expected to complete 13.9 years (UNDP, 2023), roughly a full secondary education plus a year of tertiary study. Gross tertiary enrolment is 36.4%.

Human development

The United Nations Development Programme puts Indonesia’s Human Development Index at 0.728 for 2023, in the “high human development” group. The index combines life expectancy (71.3 years), schooling (9.2 mean years, 13.9 expected) and income (GNI per capita $4,910). Indonesia graduated from lower-middle to upper-middle income status in July 2023, but its HDI still trails Malaysia (0.807) and Thailand (0.803) — the gap is mostly years of schooling.

Why it matters. With 68% of the population of working age, each extra year of schooling is the cheapest growth Indonesia can buy. See Demographics and Economy.
