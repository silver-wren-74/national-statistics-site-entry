How long Indonesians live

Life expectancy at birth was 71.3 years in 2023 (World Bank), up from about 66 years in 2000. That is below the East Asia and Pacific average of 76.9 years but close to the Southeast Asian average of 71.9 years. Infant mortality has fallen to 16 per 1,000 live births and under-5 mortality to 19 per 1,000 — roughly a third of their 2000 levels.

Universal coverage

Indonesia runs one of the world’s largest single-payer health insurance schemes. Jaminan Kesehatan Nasional (JKN), run by BPJS Kesehatan, covered about 281.8 million people in 2024 — roughly 98% of the population. Maternal mortality, at 173 deaths per 100,000 live births in WHO’s 2020 estimate, remains the indicator furthest from regional peers and the focus of most public-health spending.

Why it matters. A fertility rate of 2.12 children per woman — just above replacement — and a median age of 31 mean the disease burden is shifting from infectious diseases of childhood towards the chronic diseases of adults. See Demographics.
